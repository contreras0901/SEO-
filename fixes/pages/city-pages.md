### Body (about 900 words)

San Diego is not one wedding market. It is five or six, and the venue you choose decides your timeline, your budget shape, your weather risk, and your vendor list before a single design decision is made. We plan, design, and build florals for weddings across the county, and this is how we think about it with couples at the first meeting.

#### Downtown and the Gaslamp edge

**The Westgate Hotel.** European formality is the native language of the room. The Versailles Ballroom is about 2,550 square feet with Moscatelli chandeliers and handcrafted paneling, and sources list it between 150 seated and 250 at full capacity, so confirm the number for your layout with the hotel. It suits black-tie, Old World, and cultural ceremonies that want gilt and crystal rather than ocean. We built the florals and rentals for Cherine and Andy's Persian wedding here: [read it → `/westgate-hotel-wedding-florals-cherine-and-andy`].

**THE US GRANT.** Four ballrooms and roughly 33,000 square feet of event space. The Presidential Ballroom is listed at up to 900 for a reception, the Bivouac at about 300, Palm Court and the Crystal Ballroom at around 250. This is the downtown choice when the guest count is large and the family wants a grand hotel with a long history.

*What we plan around downtown:* load-in windows and freight elevators for florals and rentals, valet and rideshare queues on Saturday nights, and the walk between ceremony and reception rooms inside the hotel, which is where a timeline usually slips.

#### North County coast and the resorts

**Park Hyatt Aviara, Carlsbad.** About 78,000 square feet of indoor and outdoor space above the Batiquitos Lagoon. The resort requires a professional wedding planner alongside its onsite specialist, and it keeps a list of planners it has worked with. We planned, designed, and built the florals for Erica and Patrick's coastal garden wedding here, Palm Court ceremony and reception in the Gardens: [read it → `/park-hyatt-aviara-wedding-erica-and-patrick`].

**Fairmont Grand Del Mar.** The largest luxury canvas in the county: the Grand Ballroom at up to 650 seated, Grand Social at around 380 seated, Capella at about 240 seated, the Aria Pavilion and Lawn for 350-plus. Mediterranean architecture and canyon views. Design here is about restraint, because the building already carries the formality.

**The Lodge at Torrey Pines, La Jolla.** Covered on the La Jolla page.

*What we plan around in North County:* the drive. Guests staying downtown are 30 to 45 minutes from Carlsbad or Del Mar on a Saturday afternoon, so shuttle timing and a generous cocktail hour are part of the plan, not an afterthought.

Some of the best weddings we have done were at a home. A private estate gives you a blank, personal setting and takes away every piece of infrastructure a hotel provides: power, restrooms, kitchen, parking, lighting, a rain plan. That is where planning earns its keep. #### FAQ (mark each as H3; these are real buyer questions)

- **Do I need a planner at these venues?** Park Hyatt Aviara and Hotel del Coronado both state that a professional planner is required, and the Grande Colonial in La Jolla requires a private coordinator. Most luxury hotels will at minimum ask for a month-of coordinator. Confirm with the venue's catering manager before you sign.
- **What does a San Diego wedding planner cost?** Local directories quote a range of roughly $2,500 to $8,500 and up for planning, with full-service at the top. We quote after a consultation because scope drives price. `[LEDGER: confirm Bella Mia is comfortable citing a directory range; delete the sentence if not.]`
- **Can we do a beach ceremony?** On City of San Diego beaches and shoreline parks, any setup, chairs, an arch, music, needs a Park Use Permit; one wedding per day per site; no permits on major holidays. Coronado is covered in its own guide.

### Body (about 850 words)

Coronado is a village with three resort weddings and a beach inside it, and every couple who calls us about it has already fallen for one of them. Our job is not to sell you Coronado. It is to make sure the wedding you picture is the one the property can deliver on your date, at your guest count, within your budget. Here is what we tell couples.

#### Hotel del Coronado

The Del opened in 1888, sits on 28 oceanfront acres, and has been a National Historic Landmark since 1977. Ceremony options run from the Windsor Lawn to private sections of the beach to the smaller Garden Patio and Vista Walk. The Crown Room, with its 33-foot sugar-pine ceiling and crown chandeliers, is the reception most couples picture. Largest indoor space about 900 seated; largest outdoor about 1,500. Two things we plan around: it is a resort at resort volume, so on a Saturday you will not be the only event on site, and the hotel states that a planner from its own partner list is required. If you are working with a planner not on that list, confirm the property's requirements before you sign the venue contract.

#### Loews Coronado Bay Resort

The property we recommend most often to couples who want Coronado without the crowds. It sits on its own 15-acre peninsula with water on effectively every side. The Commodore Ballroom is about 13,764 square feet, pillar-free, with a foyer that overlooks the bay for cocktail hour. The Constellation Ballroom, around 5,162 square feet, has floor-to-ceiling windows and a private terrace. The Pointe is a lawn with a gazebo on the bay, our usual recommendation for an intimate ceremony. 440 rooms and 40 suites keep a travelling guest list under one roof. The single most important fact: **outdoor music stops at 10 PM** under city guidelines, so the timeline is built backwards from that moment and the move indoors is designed as a change of mood, not a shuffle.

#### Coronado Island Marriott Resort

The bay-and-skyline view. The Bayside Lawn seats up to 350 for a ceremony at the water's edge; the Skyline Terrace, tented, up to 470; the Coronado Ballroom up to about 1,000; a mid-size ballroom around 450 with a foyer over the bay. Twenty-one event spaces in all. Directory pricing starts around $13,000 for 50 guests. This is the Coronado choice for a large wedding that wants the city skyline across the water rather than the open ocean.

#### Coronado Community Center

The Nautilus Room: 4,950 square feet, up to 250 seated, a view of Glorietta Bay, a full catering kitchen, and a city facility fee instead of a resort minimum. Outside catering and rentals are the norm here, which is exactly where our styling and rental collection does its best work.

#### The beach

Small ceremonies, 20 guests or fewer with no chairs, arch, decor, or music, do not need a permit at designated areas of Coronado Beach, Centennial Park, and Bayview Park. Anything larger, or any setup at all, needs a City of Coronado wedding permit. Wind off the water is a given: structured florals, weighted linens, and a hair plan.

#### FAQ

- **Does Hotel del Coronado require a wedding planner?** The hotel states that a planner from its partner list is required for rehearsal, ceremony, and reception. Confirm current policy with the hotel.
- **What time does music have to stop in Coronado?** Outdoor music at Loews Coronado Bay stops at 10 PM under city guidelines. Indoor music continues. Other venues follow the same city rules; ask each one.
- **Can we marry on Coronado Beach?** Yes. Twenty guests or fewer with no setup needs no permit at designated areas; larger or with any setup needs a City of Coronado permit.
- **Is Coronado more expensive than San Diego?** The resorts carry resort minimums and service charges that differ indoor versus outdoor. Ask for the fully loaded number, not the package sticker, before comparing.

### Body (about 900 words)

La Jolla has the ocean the whole county wants and the rules to match. The best ceremony sites are public, permitted one wedding a day, and strict about who may set foot on them with a chair. The best receptions are a short walk away in hotels that have been doing this for a century. Here is how we match couples to the right one.

#### Scripps Seaside Forum

The oceanfront room on the Scripps Institution of Oceanography campus, next to the pier, with a lawn and patio over the Pacific. Maximum 220 guests. Everything, tables, chairs, linens, decor, comes from outside vendors, and every vendor must be licensed and insured to the campus's requirements. Vendors have one hour to strike at the end. Directory pricing starts around $9,800 for 50 guests. This is the venue where in-house florals and our own rental collection save the most friction, because there is nothing on site to fall back on.

#### Estancia La Jolla Hotel & Spa

Spanish-style, gardens and lawns. The Olive Lawn seats about 130 for a ceremony; the Garden Courtyard up to 420. The La Jolla Ballroom takes up to 420 and divides into as many as seven rooms, with a connecting patio and lawn; the Grande Room about 180. Two dressing suites on site. The ceremony fee includes chairs, a sound system, and a water station. For a mid-size to large wedding that wants gardens without a beach, this is usually the first property we show.

#### La Valencia Hotel

The pink hotel on Prospect Street, 1926. Capacity tops out around 175, with the Veranda Ballroom, Salon and Terraza for banquets, the Garden for ceremonies up to 150, and the Mediterranean Patio and Galeria for 50 to 150. Intimate, historic, ocean-view, and a short walk to the Cove and the Wedding Bowl for photographs.

#### The Lodge at Torrey Pines

Craftsman architecture above the 18th green. The Arroyo Terrace, about 10,000 square feet, seats up to 220 for a ceremony; the Maurice Braun Ballroom seats 160 to 220 for a reception. The classic flow is cocktails on the terrace at sunset and dinner in the ballroom.

#### Darlington House

A 1925 Spanish house run by a women's club, one wedding a day. The Egyptian Fountain Patio seats about 100 for a ceremony; the Rose Garden up to roughly 175 to 200 for dinner and dancing; the Andalusian Patio and Library for cocktails. Outside vendors throughout, which again is where a one-team floral, styling, and rental approach matters.

#### Grande Colonial

Boutique, Prospect Street, up to 120 guests, ceremony sites from 25 to 120 in the Loft, Grand Terrace, and Garden Courtyard. The hotel requires every wedding to have a private wedding coordinator. Directory pricing starts around $5,400 for 50 guests.

#### The Wedding Bowl and the Cove

Cuvier Park, called the Wedding Bowl, is the most popular ceremony spot on the La Jolla coastline. It is permitted for ceremonies only, not receptions, one permit a day, up to four hours including setup and strike. Ellen Browning Scripps Park and the Cove sit beside it. We pair a Wedding Bowl ceremony with a reception at La Valencia or the Grande Colonial, both within walking distance, so guests never get in a car.

#### FAQ

- **Do we need a permit for a La Jolla Cove or Wedding Bowl ceremony?** Yes, a City of San Diego Park Use Permit, one per day per site, ceremonies only, up to four hours including setup.
- **Which La Jolla venues require a planner?** The Grande Colonial requires a private coordinator; Scripps requires licensed and insured vendors for everything brought on site. Most hotels ask for at least month-of coordination.
- **What is the biggest wedding La Jolla can hold?** Estancia, at up to 420 in the Garden Courtyard and ballroom. Scripps and the Lodge top out around 220.
- **Where do guests stay?** La Valencia, the Grande Colonial, Estancia, and the Lodge are all venues and hotels. For a Scripps or Darlington wedding, we block rooms at one of them.
