Wedding search is image-driven — Google Images and Pinterest are both real referral channels in this category, and both read filenames and alt text.

### The rule, applied before every upload

**Filename:**
```
venue-name-city-wedding-couple-firstnames-photographer-##.jpg
```
Example: `hotel-del-coronado-wedding-cherine-andy-khoa-photography-12.jpg`

All lowercase. Hyphens, not underscores or spaces. No `IMG_`, no `Screenshot`, ever.

**Alt text:** describe the image as you would to someone who cannot see it, with the venue named once naturally.
```
Bride and groom exchanging vows on Windsor Lawn at Hotel del Coronado
```
Not: `wedding photo san diego wedding planner coronado wedding` — keyword stuffing in alt text is a spam signal.

### Getting Married at Hotel del Coronado: A Planner's Guide

There is no venue in San Diego that carries more weight than The Del. Couples arrive at it the way they arrive at few other properties — already emotionally committed, often before they have seen a single floor plan. Our job as planners usually is not to sell you on it. It is to make sure the wedding you imagine is the wedding the property can actually deliver on your date, at your guest count, within your budget.

Here is what we tell couples considering it.

#### The property, briefly

Hotel del Coronado sits on 28 oceanfront acres and has been a National Historic Landmark since 1977. It opened in 1888, which means you are planning inside a building with its own gravity — the Queen Anne architecture, the red turret, the sugar-pine ceilings. That is an enormous design asset and a real design constraint. A wedding that fights the building loses.

#### Ceremony spaces worth knowing

**Windsor Lawn.** Landscaped, framed by palms, running toward the water's edge. It is the classic choice for a reason, and it photographs beautifully in late afternoon light.

**The beach — Main Beach and North Beach.** The Del holds private sections of Coronado Beach. Main Beach is roughly 7,590 square feet; North Beach runs to about 70,000. Toes in the sand is genuinely available here in a way it is not at most hotels.

**Vista Walk and the Garden Patio.** Smaller, more contained, and the right call for an intimate ceremony that still wants the property in frame.

**Paseo Lawn.** Ocean views in a more relaxed register. We often use it for cocktail hour rather than ceremony.

#### Reception spaces

**The Crown Room** is the room people picture. A 33-foot rib-vaulted sugar pine ceiling, hung with crown-shaped chandeliers designed by L. Frank Baum — the author of *The Wizard of Oz*, who spent time at the hotel. It is the most photographed interior on the property and it does not need much from you. Restraint reads as luxury in that room.

**The Ocean Ballroom** brings crystal chandeliers, a built-in stage, and Pacific views. The **Coronet Room** offers floor-to-ceiling windows under a vaulted wooden ceiling. **The Garden**, at roughly 10,764 square feet, is enclosed by the Victorian architecture and works for receptions that want to stay outdoors.

#### What we plan around

**Scale.** The Del's largest indoor space seats up to 900; the largest outdoor space handles up to 1,500. The property has 757 guest rooms. This is a resort operating at resort volume, and on a busy Saturday you will not be the only wedding on site. That is not a flaw, but it changes how we build your timeline. We plan arrival routes, photography windows, and guest movement specifically so your wedding feels private inside a property that is not.

**Booking window.** The hotel's own event team has run weddings booked anywhere from one month to three years out. For a Saturday in peak season with a preferred ceremony space, plan on securing the date twelve to eighteen months ahead.

**Outside planners.** The Del maintains a short list of planning companies it recommends to couples. If you are working with a planner not on that list, confirm the property's requirements before you sign your venue contract, not after.

**Wind and sound.** Oceanfront ceremonies need amplification planning and a hair-and-makeup conversation that accounts for onshore wind. Both are solvable. Neither solves itself.

**Service charges.** Packages are quoted with food and beverage, and service charges run differently for indoor versus outdoor events. Ask for the fully loaded number before you compare The Del against anywhere else, because the sticker and the total are not the same figure.

#### Designing for the building

The mistake we see most often is a couple importing a Pinterest board built at a blank-box venue. The Del already has a point of view. The weddings that work there either lean into the Old Hollywood register — deep colour, candlelight, structured florals in the Crown Room — or go fully coastal and let the architecture stay in the background. What does not work is a third aesthetic layered over the top of the first two.

### Getting Married at Loews Coronado Bay Resort: A Planner's Guide

Loews Coronado Bay is the property we recommend most often to couples who want Coronado without the crowds. It sits on its own 15-acre peninsula, surrounded by water on effectively every side — bay, marina, ocean, and the San Diego skyline across the water. Unlike a resort on a main strip, you are not sharing your sightlines with day visitors.

#### Why planners like this property

**Scale without compromise.** The Commodore Ballroom offers 13,764 square feet of pillar-free space with nearly 16-foot ceilings, divisible into five sections. It is the largest ballroom in Coronado. Pillar-free matters more than couples expect: it means your room layout is not negotiating around columns, and no guest table has an obstructed view of your first dance.

**A genuine cocktail-hour solution.** The Commodore's foyer overlooks San Diego Bay. Your cocktail hour is not in a hallway — it is a windowed space with a view, immediately adjacent to the reception room. That adjacency removes one of the most common timeline risks at a large wedding: moving 200 guests between buildings.

**Range.** The resort runs weddings from very small to very large — spaces across the property handle roughly 25 guests up to a thousand. The **Constellation Ballroom** brings floor-to-ceiling windows, crystal chandeliers, crown molding, and a private terrace over the marina and pools, at about 5,162 square feet, divisible in two. **The Avalon** offers windows on every wall with views of the bay, the downtown skyline, and the Coronado Bridge. **The Pointe** is a semi-private outdoor lawn with a small gazebo, hugged by the bay and marina — our usual recommendation for a first look or an intimate ceremony.

**Cultural celebrations.** The property regularly hosts multi-day and multi-event weddings, including South Asian celebrations across several spaces, and accommodates outside catering. If your wedding runs over three days rather than one afternoon, this resort is built for it.

**Accommodation.** 440 rooms and 40 suites on site. For a wedding where most guests are travelling, having the entire party under one roof removes an enormous amount of transport planning — and removes the late-night driving risk that comes with it.

#### What we plan around

**The 10 PM outdoor music cutoff.** Per city guidelines, outdoor music at the resort stops at 10 PM. This is the single most important scheduling fact about this venue and the one couples discover too late. It does not mean your wedding ends at ten. It means your timeline has to be built backwards from it: ceremony, dinner service, toasts, and first dances all sized so that the outdoor portion finishes cleanly and the celebration moves indoors without feeling like it was cut off. We build that transition deliberately, usually as a deliberate change of mood rather than a shuffle.

**Weather backup.** The resort reserves an indoor backup room for outdoor events. Confirm which room, and walk it, before you finalise your design. A backup space you have never seen is not a plan.

**Peninsula access.** One road in. For a large wedding with a shuttle, build extra minutes into every transport leg. Nobody remembers a timeline that ran five minutes early. Everyone remembers a ceremony that started late.

**Wind off the water.** Same conversation as anywhere on Coronado. Structured florals, weighted linens, and a hair plan that accounts for it.

#### Designing here

This property is far more neutral than The Del, and that is its advantage. The renovated interiors run to soft, beach-neutral tones, which means your palette can be almost anything and the room will not argue with it. Couples who want a specific, personal design — a particular colour story, a cultural tradition given real space, a reception that looks like them rather than like the venue — usually find more freedom here.
