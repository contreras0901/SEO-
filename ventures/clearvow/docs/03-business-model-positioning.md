# Deliverable 3: Recommended business model and competitive positioning

| Model | Demand | Startup cost | Acquisition difficulty | Margin | Competition | Defensibility | Scalability | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| National marketplace (Knot clone) | High | Very high | Extreme (The Knot, Zola) | Good at scale | Brutal | Low for a newcomer | High | Reject |

## 3. Ideal customers

**Couples (demand side).** Engaged couples 12 to 18 months out, planning a $15k to $75k wedding in one metro, who have been burned by "request pricing" and want to shortlist fast. The product treats every couple identically: two partner records, each with name and optional pronouns, no gendered roles anywhere in the data model. Same-sex and non-binary couples get a visible "inclusive pledge" filter and vendors who have signed it.

**Vendors (supply side), in priority order of willingness to pay.**
1. Photographers, videographers, DJs and bands, planners, florists. Many sole proprietors, inquiry-driven, already paying The Knot or WeddingWire, and most vocal about lead quality.
2. Caterers, rentals, beauty, officiants, cake, stationery, transportation, luxury restroom trailers.
3. Venues. High value, slow to buy, want "Spotlight" and photo-rich real weddings. Venues publish "site fee from" and "per-guest from" prices.

The founder's own companies (Bella Mia Exclusive Events, Ethereal Luxury Restrooms) are vendor number one and two. They are also a conflict of interest to manage: see Deliverable 12.

## 4. Core value proposition

- **To couples:** "See the price before you ask. Contact only vendors you can afford, who respond, and who welcome you."
- **To vendors:** "Inquiries that are real, with the details you need, from couples who already know your prices. No contracts. Cancel anytime."

## 5. Positioning statement

For engaged couples and the professionals who serve them, Clearvow is the wedding marketplace that publishes prices, verifies every inquiry, and welcomes every couple. Unlike The Knot, it never sells unverified leads or 12-month contracts. Unlike inspiration blogs, it is built to get you booked.

## 6. Why this model wins

1. **It attacks the incumbents' revenue model, not their product.** The Knot cannot publish prices or verify inquiries without shrinking the vendor spend that funds it. That is a structural weakness, not a feature gap.
2. **It uses an asset competitors do not have in San Diego:** the founder's vendor relationships, venue relationships, real-wedding content, and an SEO practice (Atelier) that already ranks local wedding pages.
3. **It makes content do double duty.** Each real wedding recruits five to thirteen vendors, each with prices, and ranks for venue-name queries that couples search with high intent.
4. **It earns the right to the second product.** Vendors who run their inquiry inbox on Clearvow will adopt proposals, contracts, and deposits in year 2 to 3, which creates switching costs.

## 7. Sustainable competitive advantage (ordered by realism)

| Advantage | Achievable by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Metro density and local brand ("the San Diego one with prices") | Month 12 | Realistic with founder network and content |
| Proprietary price and quote-outcome dataset | Month 18 | Starts the day vendors publish prices; compounds with "did the quote match" feedback |
| Verified inquiry reputation among vendors | Month 12 | Word of mouth in vendor Facebook groups is fast and brutal in both directions |
| Vendor workflow lock-in (proposals, contracts, payments) | Month 30 | Requires year-2 build |
| Network effects across metros | Month 36+ | Only after four to six metros |
| Brand authority nationally | Aspiration | Not a 36-month goal |

## 8. Flaws in the original brief, and what we did about them

- "Build something very similar" to the four sites. We did not. A similar site loses on day one to the incumbents. We built around what they will not do.
- "Brides, grooms, and same-sex couples" as the framing. Inclusivity is a design standard here, not a segment strategy. Making it the headline would narrow the market and invite the comparison with Equally Wed, which already owns that story.
- A "$100 million company" vision with a "powerhouse platform." The marketplace that gets there is boring at launch: one city, one promise, a hundred vendors who keep their word on prices. The roadmap in Deliverable 10 scales from there and the aggressive scenario in Deliverable 4 shows what the upside looks like.
