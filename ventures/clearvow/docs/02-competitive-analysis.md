## 7. Where competitors can and cannot be beaten

**Can be beaten (in a metro, within 12 months):** price transparency, inquiry quality, vendor trust, response accountability, inclusive defaults, local content depth (venue price guides, real weddings with full vendor credits), and vendor cost (no contracts, lower price than The Knot's metro placement).

**Cannot be beaten (within 36 months):** national vendor count, review volume, registry and website lock-in, brand awareness, and ChatGPT distribution. Clearvow should never market itself as "the new Knot." It should be "the marketplace where you see the price before you ask."

## 8. Market facts and trends we rely on

- US weddings: about 2 million per year (The Knot 2026 study, VERIFIED company figure). Average spend $34,000; 13 professionals per wedding.
- San Diego: no official annual count. REPORTED guesses range from ~24,000 (a vendor blog, unsourced) to ~30,000 (an old bridal-show organizer estimate). ASSUMPTION for the model: 20,000 to 30,000 weddings per year in the county. San Diego average cost REPORTED between $37,690 and $49,473 depending on source.
- Same-sex weddings: REPORTED ~59,000 per year on average since 2015 (Williams Institute, 591,000 over ten years), 80% with a celebration, average $8,546 spend, 48 guests. Smaller spend per wedding, strongly underserved on inclusivity.
- Pricing transparency demand: REPORTED 78% of couples say pricing is the top factor deciding whom to contact (WeddingPro figure, primary source not located); 37% of couples contacted more vendors than intended (The Knot 2026 study, via a secondary source).
- AI: REPORTED 36% of couples use AI tools for planning (The Knot, Feb 2026), nearly double early 2025.
- Vendor trust crisis: VERIFIED press coverage of fake-lead allegations and FTC attention through 2025.
