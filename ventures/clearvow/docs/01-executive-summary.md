## The one-paragraph version

The four reference sites are not one business. The Knot is a national lead-selling machine, Loverly and Carats + Cake are vendor-subscription directories wrapped in inspiration content, and Wedding Sparrow is a boutique fine-art magazine. Cloning or merging them is the wrong move: the national directory game is already won by The Knot Worldwide (REPORTED ~200,000 US vendors, 14.6M reviews, a ChatGPT app since February 2026), and the inspiration-blog game has thin margins. The opening is different. Couples say pricing is the top factor in deciding whom to contact (REPORTED 78%, WeddingPro, secondary citation), yet almost no directory publishes prices. Vendors are in open revolt against pay-to-play lead quality (VERIFIED: 2025 New Yorker and PetaPixel reporting, ~200 complaints relayed to the FTC by Senator Grassley's office in October 2025). Nobody owns "the honest marketplace." Clearvow should be **the price-transparent, inclusive-by-default wedding marketplace, launched one metro at a time, starting in San Diego where the founder already has a planner, floral, rental, and restroom-trailer business, hotel relationships, and an SEO practice.** Vendors pay a flat, no-contract subscription or unlock individual verified inquiries. Couples pay nothing. Real weddings, credited vendor by vendor, are the content and data engine.

## The three most promising opportunities (ranked)

1. **Price-transparent local marketplace with verified inquiries (recommended).** Vendors publish starting prices and packages as a condition of being listed in the main directory. Couples submit structured inquiries (date, venue, guest count, budget band) that are verified by email and phone before a vendor sees them. Vendors pay a flat subscription or per-inquiry unlock, never a 12-month contract. Defensibility comes from pricing data no one else has, from a vendor reputation signal ("responds within 24h, price-honest") that couples learn to trust, and from metro-by-metro density.
2. **Vendor operating tools layered on the marketplace (year 2 to 3).** Once vendors live in Clearvow's inquiry inbox, add proposals, contracts, deposits, and a shared couple-vendor timeline. HoneyBook's 2025 price increase (REPORTED Starter $19 to $36/mo) and its 1.5% payment surcharge left room for a wedding-specific, cheaper tool bundled with distribution. This is the switching-cost layer.
3. **Inclusive wedding content and credential network.** An "every couple" standard (partner A / partner B fields, pronouns, no "bride" defaults, a vetted inclusivity pledge like Equally Wed's, surfaced as a filter) is a real but secondary opportunity. The same-sex wedding segment is meaningful (REPORTED Williams Institute: ~591,000 same-sex marriages in the ten years after Obergefell, 80% held a celebration) but it is roughly 1 to 2% of US weddings. It should be a structural feature of the whole product, not the brand's only hook. We challenge the premise that it is a standalone market: Equally Wed has owned the niche since 2010 with a 2 to 10 person team, which tells you the ceiling of a niche-only play.

## Recommended winning model

A **hybrid local marketplace**: vendor SaaS subscriptions (flat, cancel anytime) plus pay-per-verified-inquiry for free-tier vendors, plus a small number of capped "Spotlight" placements per city and category. Couples never pay. Content (real weddings, venue guides, price guides) is produced to rank and to credit vendors, which recruits vendors for free. Launch in San Diego only, with a target of 150 claimed vendor profiles and 20 paying vendors in 90 days before spending on a second metro.

## What makes the platform materially superior

- **Published prices on every listed vendor.** Starting price, typical range, and at least one package. The Knot, Loverly, Carats + Cake, and Wedding Sparrow show none (VERIFIED on the pages fetched).
- **Verified inquiries, not leads.** Every inquiry carries a confirmed email, a confirmed phone, a date, a venue or city, a guest count, and a budget band. Vendors see the whole inquiry before paying to unlock it on the free tier. This is the direct answer to the fake-lead scandal.
- **No contracts.** Monthly or annual, cancel in one click. Loverly already does this; The Knot does not.
- **Inclusive by construction.** Partner fields, pronouns, and an inclusivity pledge with a vendor-facing checklist, all day one.
- **Every real wedding is a credit graph.** Each published wedding links every vendor to a profile, with prices visible, so content becomes acquisition for both sides.
- **Response-time and honesty signals.** Median response time and "price matched the quote" feedback are shown on profiles. Couples can rate whether the final quote matched the published range.

## The fastest credible route to revenue

Month 1 to 3, San Diego only:

1. Seed 150 vendor profiles from the founder's own network (Bella Mia's past vendor teams: photographers, DJs, caterers, venues like Westgate, Park Hyatt Aviara, La Valencia, Loews Coronado) plus public listings, each invited to claim and publish prices.
2. Publish 25 real weddings and 10 venue price guides written for search ("Park Hyatt Aviara wedding cost", "San Diego wedding photographer prices").
3. Open Pro at $59/mo (annual $590) with a founding-vendor rate of $39/mo locked for life for the first 50. Twenty paying vendors is $1,000 to $1,200 MRR and validates willingness to pay.
4. Run a $500/mo paid search test on "San Diego wedding [category] prices" queries to measure couple inquiry cost.

The decision gate to spend on metro two is: 20 paying vendors, 100 verified inquiries delivered, vendor-reported inquiry quality of 4 of 5 or better, and a measured cost per verified inquiry under $25.

## Honest assessment of the risks

- **Chicken-and-egg.** Vendors want couples, couples want vendors. Mitigation: content and the founder's network supply the first vendors; SEO on price queries supplies the first couples; a single metro keeps density high.
- **The Knot's response.** If The Knot publishes prices, Clearvow's wedge narrows. INFERENCE: The Knot's revenue depends on vendors paying for placement, and vendors resist publishing prices, so a Knot pivot would take years and is fought internally. Still, speed matters.
- **Vendor resistance to publishing prices.** Many vendors believe published prices lose them deals. Mitigation: "starting at" plus a range, plus the data showing that couples who see prices inquire more seriously. Vendors who refuse can hold a free, unlisted profile that still receives credits from real weddings.
- **Founder bandwidth.** The founder runs three businesses. This venture needs a product owner. See Deliverable 12.
