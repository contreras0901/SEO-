# Deliverable 12: The five strategic decisions that determine success or failure

## 1. Require published prices, or merely encourage them

The entire positioning depends on "every listed vendor shows a price." The moment the directory allows "contact for pricing," Clearvow becomes Loverly with fewer vendors. The cost is supply: some vendors, especially luxury planners and some venues, will refuse. Recommendation: require it, without exception, and give refusers a free unlisted profile that still collects real-wedding credits. Measure what share refuse in Stage 1. If more than half of the high-value vendors refuse, the business still works at the mid-market, which is where most of the 2 million US weddings happen.

## 2. One metro deep, or many metros thin

A national directory with three vendors per category per city is worthless to couples and vendors alike, and it hands The Knot the comparison it wants. Recommendation: San Diego only until the Stage 2 gate, then one metro at a time with a launch partner. The temptation to "launch nationally because the code supports it" is the single most likely way to waste the first year.

## 3. How to handle the founder's own vendors on the platform

Bella Mia and Ethereal will be listed. If they get Spotlight slots, favorable ranking, or early access to inquiries, vendor trust collapses the first time someone notices, and in a small market someone will. Recommendation: list them under identical rules, ban them from Spotlight, publish that policy on the About page (already written), and log every admin action touching them in the audit log (already implemented). Decide now whether the founder's planner business will even accept inquiries through Clearvow in year one; it may be cleaner to list it as unclaimed-but-credited.

## 4. Verification friction versus inquiry volume

Email plus SMS verification is friction. It will cut inquiry volume, perhaps by 20 to 40%. It is also the proof behind "inquiries, not leads." Recommendation: keep both, measure the drop-off at the verify step weekly, and reduce friction with Google sign-in and a one-tap SMS link in Stage 2 rather than by removing verification. If vendor-reported inquiry quality is below 4 of 5 even with verification, the problem is elsewhere (budget bands too wide, vendors too slow), not the verification.

## 5. Marketplace only, or marketplace plus vendor tools

Vendor tools (proposals, contracts, deposits) create switching costs and raise ARPU, but they are a second product with their own competitors and compliance burden. Build them too early and the marketplace starves; never build them and Pro churns to whoever owns the vendor's workflow. Recommendation: do not write a line of vendor-tools code before 300 live vendors and clear survey demand, then build them as extensions of the inquiry object rather than as a separate app.

## Open tasks that gate launch (not decisions, but blockers)

- Trademark and domain search for "Clearvow"; fallback names in Deliverable 5.
- Counsel review of terms, privacy, vendor terms, review policy, and takedown process.
- Stripe account, products, and webhook; Resend and Twilio accounts; Neon database; Vercel project.
- Image upload to object storage (the MVP accepts hosted image URLs only).
- A real person named as the product owner with at least 15 hours a week. The founder's existing three businesses are the main risk to this one.
