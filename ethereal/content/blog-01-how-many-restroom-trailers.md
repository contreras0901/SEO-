# How Many Restroom Trailers Do I Need for My Event?

This is the first question almost every client asks us, and it is the right one. Too few stalls means a line. Too many means you paid for space nobody used. This post explains what drives restroom use at an event, how our trailer sizes map to that, and what to tell us so we can recommend a size.

Short version: the number of guests matters, but event length, alcohol, and meals matter nearly as much.

## Start with the guest count, then adjust

Guest count is the starting point, not the answer. Two events with 120 guests can need different setups. A two-hour afternoon reception with light snacks is one thing. A seven-hour wedding with cocktails, dinner, and an open bar is another.

Here is the general industry framing. These are general statements, not our specific sizing rules.

- **Longer events increase usage.** Every hour adds more trips per guest. An all-day event needs more capacity than a short one even with the same headcount.
- **Alcohol increases usage.** An open bar or beer and wine service raises restroom traffic noticeably. Plan for more capacity when drinks are served.
- **Meals increase usage.** A seated dinner or a long buffet creates a wave of restroom trips in the hour after people eat.
- **Guest mix matters.** Older guests, families with small children, and guests who need an accessible stall all change what you should rent.
- **Peaks matter more than averages.** Restroom demand is not spread evenly. It spikes right after the ceremony, after dinner, and during breaks in a program. Size for the spike, not the average.

## One larger trailer or two smaller ones?

Sometimes a client asks whether two 2-stall trailers are better than one 4-stall. There are real tradeoffs.

One larger trailer takes one delivery, one placement spot, and one set of hookups. It is simpler. Two smaller trailers can be split between two areas, for example one near the ceremony site and one near the reception, which can reduce walking and spread out the crowd.

## Questions we will ask you

When you [request a quote](/book-now), we size the trailer from a few details. Having these ready speeds things up.

1. How many guests do you expect?
2. How long is the event, from first arrival to last departure?
3. Will alcohol be served? Open bar or limited?
4. Is there a seated meal?
5. Are there other restrooms on site that guests can also use?
6. Do any guests need an accessible stall?
7. Is the trailer the only restroom, or a supplement to the venue's?

That last question matters. A trailer that supplements a venue's indoor restrooms can be smaller than one that is the only option at a backyard wedding.

## Common sizing mistakes

**Sizing for the invite list instead of the RSVP count.** Use your expected attendance, not the number of invitations sent.

**Ignoring vendors and staff.** Caterers, photographers, musicians, and planners use the restroom too. Add them to your count.

**Forgetting the timeline.** An event that starts with a ceremony and ends with a late-night dance floor runs long. Count the real hours.

## Weddings, corporate, and backyard events

Each type has its own pattern.

- **Weddings** run long, usually include a meal and drinks, and have a sharp peak after the ceremony. See [wedding restrooms](/wedding-restrooms).
- **Corporate events and galas** often have a program with scheduled breaks, which creates predictable surges. See [corporate events](/corporate-events).
- **Backyard parties and quinceañeras** are usually all-ages, run late, and often have no other restroom option except inside the house.
