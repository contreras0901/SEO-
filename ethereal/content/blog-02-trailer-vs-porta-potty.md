# Restroom Trailer vs. Porta Potty: Which One Does Your Event Need?

Both solve the same problem. Guests need a restroom and the site does not have enough. The difference is in what the guest experiences, what the site needs to support it, and what it costs. This post lays out the tradeoffs without pretending a porta potty is never the right answer. Sometimes it is.

## What a porta potty is

A standard portable toilet is a single plastic cabin with a non-flushing toilet over a holding tank. Some have a hand sanitizer dispenser. Some upgraded units have a small sink. There is no running water, no climate control, no mirror of any size, and no lighting beyond what comes through the translucent roof.

Porta potties are cheap, easy to place, and need nothing from the site. That is why they are everywhere on construction sites and at large outdoor festivals.

## What a restroom trailer is

A restroom trailer is a towable unit built out like an indoor bathroom. Ours have flushing toilets, running water at the sinks, climate control, and finished interiors. Guests walk up steps, open a door, and step into a room that looks and feels like a bathroom in a nice home or hotel. There are private stalls, real sinks, mirrors, and lighting.

We offer 2-, 3-, and 4-stall trailers and a 5-stall ADA trailer with an accessible stall. Each comes in the **Cortessa** design line, with marble-inspired finishes and statement lighting, or the **Standard** line, with clean, classic styling. See them on the [restroom trailers page](/restrooms) and the [design options page](/design-options).

## When a porta potty is the right call

Be honest with yourself about the event.

- **Construction sites with a small crew.** A standard unit is often enough, and the cost difference is hard to justify. That said, we do rent solar-powered single units for construction sites, and some contractors prefer them for client-facing remodels. See [construction rentals](/construction-rentals).
- **Very large public festivals** where volume matters more than experience.
- **Short, casual gatherings** where guests will not be on site long and nobody is dressed up.
- **Sites with no access** for a trailer and no way to deliver one.

## When a restroom trailer is the right call

- **Weddings.** Guests are in formal clothes, the event runs for hours, and the restroom is part of the experience whether you want it to be or not. See [wedding restrooms](/wedding-restrooms).
- **Corporate events, galas, and brand activations.** The restroom reflects on the host. See [corporate events](/corporate-events).
- **Backyard celebrations** where the alternative is sending a hundred guests through your house.
- **Hot or cold weather.** Climate control is not a luxury in a Southern California summer or a cold evening in the hills.
- **Productions** where talent and crew are on location all day.
- **Emergency rentals** when a building loses its restrooms and staff or customers still need to use the facility. See [emergency rentals](/emergency-rentals).
- **Any event with older guests or guests who need an accessible stall.** The 5-stall ADA trailer handles this.

## The cost question

A porta potty costs a fraction of a trailer. That is true. The better comparison is the trailer against the rest of the event budget. Our published rates start at $1,150 for a 2-stall trailer plus delivery. Against catering, rentals, florals, and photography for a wedding, the restroom is a small line that affects every guest.

## A simple test

Ask one question. Will your guests remember the restroom?

At a construction site, nobody will. At a wedding, a gala, or a milestone birthday at home, they will, one way or the other. If the answer is yes, a trailer is worth the difference. If the answer is no, save the money.
